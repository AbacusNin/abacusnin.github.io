Crypto

Classical ciphers, weak randomness, and key-reuse bugs. Drafts below; full writeups are being added.

Repeating-key XOR and base-9 encoding

Detected a short repeating key against a known-plaintext crib, then peeled back a base-9 ASCII layer underneath. The interesting part was ordering the two: the encoding hid the key length until the outer layer came off. Full writeup coming.

LCG jackpot

A linear congruential generator seeded predictably, so recovering a few outputs was enough to reconstruct the state and roll the sequence forward. A reminder that a generator is only as strong as its unpredictability. Full writeup coming.

RSA and ECDSA nonce reuse

Two signatures sharing a nonce leak the private key through simple algebra. The defensive lesson is the one that matters: deterministic nonces exist for exactly this reason. Full writeup coming.

Patterned password hashes

Hashes of passwords that followed a fixed template collapsed the search space to almost nothing. The takeaway is about how structure in secrets becomes structure an attacker can walk. Full writeup coming.

Pigpen and Atbash

A layered classical puzzle: a pigpen substitution wrapped around an Atbash step. Mostly a warm-up, but a clean example of peeling layers in the right order. Full writeup coming.
